The 6 Steps

Solve layer by layer — no advanced concepts required.

01

Daisy

Place 4 white edge stickers around the yellow center.

 02

White Cross

Flip daisy down to form the white cross on bottom.

 03

White Corners

Solve all 4 white corner pieces.

 04

Second Layer

Insert 4 middle-layer edges.

 05

Yellow Cross

Form the yellow cross on top (Algorithm 1).

 06

Solve (OLL + PLL)

Orient + permute yellow corners and edges (Algorithms 2–7).
Step 01

Daisy

Goal: 4 white edge stickers surrounding the yellow center (top face). This is the setup for the white cross.

  • Hold the yellow center facing up.
  • Find white edge pieces (the ones with white).
  • Move each white edge to sit next to the yellow center.
  • No algorithm needed — just position them with intuition.
Look for the yellow center on top, build white petals around it.
Step 02

White Cross

Goal: Flip the daisy down to form a white cross on the bottom face (D). Align each white edge with its center color.

  • Turn each daisy edge down with F2, R2, L2, or B2.
  • Match the side sticker to the center color.
  • Repeat for all 4 edges until the cross is aligned.
Each edge's side color must line up with its matching center.
Step 03

White Corners

Goal: Solve all 4 white corners. Find each white corner piece, position it above its target slot, then repeat the right-hand algorithm until it drops in.

Right-Hand Algorithm
R U R' U'
  • Find a white corner; bring it above its correct slot on D.
  • If white faces right: do R U R' U' once or twice.
  • If white faces front: do U R U' R' once or twice.
Step 04

Second Layer

Goal: Insert 4 middle-layer edges into the second (E) layer. Use one of two "sexy-sledge" algorithms depending on edge direction.

Left Insert (edge goes left)
U' L' U L U F U' F'
Right Insert (edge goes right)
U R U' R' U' F' U F
  • Find an edge in the top layer with no yellow.
  • Match its side color to the center, note which way it points.
  • Apply the left or right insert algorithm.
Step 05

Yellow Cross

Goal: Form a yellow cross on top (U). Yellow corners don't need to be oriented yet — just the cross shape.

Algorithm 1 — Yellow Cross
F R U R' U' F'
  • Dot (0 yellow edges): do Algorithm 1 once → L-shape.
  • L-shape: orient toward top-left, do Algorithm 1 → Line.
  • Line (horizontal): do Algorithm 1 → Cross.
Step 06

Yellow Face (OLL)

Goal: Make the entire top face yellow. Two algorithms — Sune and Anti-Sune — handle every corner case.

Algorithm 2 — OLL (Sune)
RUR'URU2R'
Algorithm 3 — OLL (Anti-Sune)
R'U'RU'R'U2R
  • If yellow not all up: use Sune or Anti-Sune (Algorithms 2–3).
  • Yellow corners don't need to be in the right place yet — only facing up.
  • Rotate the top layer and repeat Sune until the whole top face is yellow.
Step 07

Permute Last Layer (PLL)

Goal: With the yellow face up, move each last-layer piece to its correct spot. Three algorithms finish the cube.

Algorithm 4 — PLL (Ua Perm)
RU'RURURU'R'U'R2
Algorithm 5 — PLL (Ub Perm)
R2URUR'U'R'U'R'UR'
Algorithm 6 — Corner Cycle (Clockwise)
R'FR'B2RF'R'B2R2
Algorithm 7 — Corner Cycle (Counter-Clockwise)
R2B2RFR'B2RF'R
  • Corners come first: turn the top layer until one corner sits in its correct place (its side colors match).
  • Hold that corner at the front-left and apply Algorithm 6 (or 7 for the opposite direction) until every corner is solved.
  • Then solve the edges: hold any solved edge at the back and use Algorithm 4 or 5 (Ua/Ub perm) until they match.
Solved! If two pieces are swapped, apply the matching algorithm once more.

Beginner Method FAQ

Direct answers to the questions new cubers ask most.

How long does it take a beginner to solve a Rubik's Cube?

Most beginners finish their first successful solve in 10–30 minutes using this method, and drop under 2 minutes after a few hours of practice. For comparison, the official world record is 2.76 seconds, set by Teodor Zajder on February 8, 2026.

What is the easiest way to solve a Rubik's Cube?

The easiest way is the beginner layer-by-layer method on this page: it breaks the solve into 6 steps and uses only about 5 short algorithms. Advanced methods like CFOP cut your time roughly in half but require memorizing 78 algorithms.

How many algorithms do you need to memorize to solve the cube?

You need about 5 algorithms for the beginner method, most of them under 8 moves long. Full CFOP requires 78 algorithms: 42 F2L cases, 57 OLL cases, and 21 PLL cases.

What do the letters mean in Rubik's Cube algorithms?

Each letter names a face you turn clockwise: U (up), D (down), L (left), R (right), F (front), B (back). An apostrophe (like R') means turn that face counterclockwise instead.
